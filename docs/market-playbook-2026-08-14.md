# Roblox revenue playbook — 2026-08-14

This document treats successful experiences as **mechanic and business-pattern evidence**, not as assets to clone. Never copy code, maps, names, characters, artwork, audio, thumbnails, text, or brand identity.

## Live market snapshot

Roblox Charts `Top Playing Now` at the observation time surfaced Murder Mystery 2 (~926K displayed concurrent), Grow a Garden 2 (~451K), Brookhaven RP (~434K), Blox Fruits (~337K), and 99 Nights in the Forest among the leaders. Live counts are volatile; only the structural patterns below are used for product decisions.

## Pattern 1 — legible loop before join

Current leaders can be explained in one short sentence. A player can predict what to do immediately. SkillCity keeps one clear fantasy: **become a respected, competent citizen by doing practical city jobs with other players.**

Release gate: first meaningful action in <= 60 seconds, first reward in <= 120 seconds.

## Pattern 2 — time and return create value

Grow a Garden 2 explicitly uses shop restocks, waiting/growth, harvest/sell, weekly guild rewards, and offline growth. These create reasons to return without requiring an endless content catalog.

SkillCity transfer:
- rotating city contracts and market inventory,
- daily/weekly city reputation goals,
- licenses/mastery that advance through demonstrated skill,
- optional neighborhood/team weekly goals,
- offline progress only for benign long-running projects, never for paid advantage.

Do **not** copy the garden, currency, theft loop, art, copy, or economy numbers.

## Pattern 3 — social adjacency beats forced grouping

Brookhaven's core description is identity + houses + vehicles + city roleplay. The environment supports social stories without making another player mandatory for basic play.

SkillCity transfer:
- free solo jobs remain productive,
- shared incidents pay a social bonus rather than blocking progress,
- visible roles, licenses, vehicles and home/garage customization create status,
- private-server controls can become a convenience/status purchase without public-server power.

## Pattern 4 — common threat creates spontaneous communication

99 Nights' public pitch is extremely small: build a camp with friends while something watches you. A shared changing threat naturally creates calls for help and division of labor.

SkillCity transfer:
- citywide incidents: blackout, school-zone rush, traffic jam, market shortage, emergency dispatch,
- different roles see complementary information,
- one person can contribute, but 2–4 players coordinating earn a team bonus,
- incidents last minutes, not hours, so failed coordination does not ruin a session.

## Pattern 5 — short resets + persistent meta

Round/event games create frequent fresh starts while persistent cosmetics/mastery preserve long-term identity. SkillCity should use 6–8 minute shifts/events inside a persistent city reputation layer.

## Pattern 6 — frequent themed updates, not giant expansions

Brookhaven's listing shows frequent themed updates and new props/vehicles. LiveOps Autopilot exists specifically to make small seasonal/event changes cheap and reversible.

Release rule: one KPI hypothesis per update; ship small, measure, keep/rollback.

## Pattern 7 — creator-tool buyers pay to remove repeated toil

Creator Store defines plugins as Studio extensions that enhance workflows and speed up the creative process. The first catalog therefore sells outcomes with immediate time savings:

1. RevenueOS — monetization readiness + fail-closed receipt scaffold.
2. QA Autopilot — localization/mobile release audit.
3. LiveOps Autopilot — Experience Config + kill-switch + messaging scaffold.

Each product must deliver first value in <3 minutes and work without support calls.

## Copycat saturation is a failure signal

Search results contain many experiences using near-identical hit names or explicitly calling themselves modded/inspired versions. That is evidence that literal cloning is an overcrowded strategy with weak defensibility and elevated IP/moderation risk.

Our rule is therefore **copy the economic/mechanical invariant, then replace the fantasy and implementation**.

Examples:
- offline growth -> persistent city projects,
- stealing risk -> time-sensitive public incidents,
- guild competition -> neighborhood/city-team reputation,
- houses/vehicles -> homes/garages/status identity,
- survival night -> citywide emergency window,
- murder round -> nonviolent role-information/decision round.

## Monetization priority

For games, status/identity/convenience precede power:
- cosmetic vehicles and body kits,
- home/garage decoration,
- role animations/emotes,
- cosmetic loadout slots,
- themed seasons,
- private-server control packs.

Never sell correct answers, essential safety knowledge, fake engagement, or basic progression viability.

For creator tools, sell a specific avoided cost: fewer setup hours, fewer release bugs, faster LiveOps, safer monetization.

## Kill conditions

Do not scale an idea because it is aesthetically good. Kill/reposition after two instrumented iterations fail the target KPI. Never buy traffic to repair weak retention, never add content volume to hide a weak core loop, and never create ten experiences before one acquisition/retention/monetization loop is understood.
